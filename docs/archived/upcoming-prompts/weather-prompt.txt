CORE EXPERIENCE

under:

- exceptional clear weather;
- scattered maritime cumulus;
- high thin cloud and humid haze;
- a low marine overcast with drizzle;
- a forceful rain squall;
- a dark convective thunderstorm.

Conditions must not be simple full-screen colour grades.

A clear day should preserve the beauty and clarity established by the graphics
round.

Broken cloud should create moving pools of Sun and shadow, changing sky

A thunderstorm should feel large, dark and powerful while retaining readable
cloud structure, water, raft silhouette and spatially localised illumination.
Lightning should briefly reveal the scene rather than behave like a white
screen flash.

CURRENT ATMOSPHERIC WIND VERSUS WIND-SEA MEMORY

This distinction is mandatory.

There are two physically different quantities:

A. CURRENT ATMOSPHERIC SURFACE WIND

Owned by the local weather system.

It represents what the air is doing now.

It immediately drives:

- gust sampling;
- cloud or layer advection where appropriate;
- rain slant and advection;
- capillary and fine surface roughness;
- local reflection/glint roughness modulation;
- active whitecap-generation gating;
- foam advection and new windrow orientation;
- spindrift transport and emission gating;
- raft-contact airborne spray transport;
- sail and loose-material presentation;
- wind and rain ambience.

B. DEVELOPED WIND-SEA MEMORY

Owned by the sea-state system.

It represents wave energy produced by wind acting over fetch and duration.

It controls the existing:

- wind-sea spectrum;
- dominant local wind-sea period;
- developed wave height;
- directional spread;
- wind-sea maturity;
- large and medium displacement felt by the raft.

It changes on sea-state timescales and through the established
`SeaStateController`/`WaveField` transition contract.

C. REMOTE SWELL

Primary and secondary swell remain independent of present local weather.

A local wind change must not:

- rotate established remote swell;
- erase post-storm swell;
- instantly create a mature large wind sea;
- reset phases;
- pop the wave field.

- increase the relative importance of diffuse sky light;
- soften or remove hard Sun shadows;
- retain directional and tonal richness;
- avoid turning every material into the same grey value.

Dense cloud should:

- attenuate stars behind it;
- dim or obscure the Sun and Moon according to optical depth;
- alter Moon halo and Sun aureole in a restrained moisture-aware manner;
- affect the light reaching the ocean.

Do not hide celestial objects with unrelated global opacity while leaving their
scene lights at full strength.

CLOUDS AND OCEAN REFLECTION

The visible sky and reflected sky must remain perceptually coherent.

Do not evaluate an unaffordable full volumetric cloud renderer inside every
ocean fragment merely for mathematical purity.

Use a measured approximation where required, such as:

- a lower-frequency analytic cloud transmittance;
- a weather environment map updated at a controlled rate;
- a bounded sky-light probe;
- a simplified reflection representation;
- another documented method.

The result must preserve:

- reflected blue gaps under broken cloud;
- darker reflected cloud masses;
- overcast sky reflection;
- cloud-dependent Sun/Moon glint;
- no obvious mismatch in direction or motion;
- no stale environment during transitions;
- no large frame-time spikes during environment updates.

ATMOSPHERE, HUMIDITY, HAZE, AND VISIBILITY

Treat the final graphics round's clear maritime atmosphere as the neutral base.

Weather may modulate:

- aerosol or turbidity;
- moisture haze;
- Sun and Moon aureoles;
- horizon contrast;
- distance extinction;
- ambient sky radiance;
- precipitation extinction.

Use a coherent distance-aware visibility model.

Heavy rain should reduce the distance at which:

- individual waves remain distinct;
- the horizon remains high contrast;
- the raft remains visible from the far camera;
- cloud bases remain crisp.

Do not implement reduced visibility as only:

- a flat full-screen grey overlay;
- arbitrary global desaturation;
- an opaque plane around the camera;
- a camera-attached radial wall.

Preserve:

- plausible sky/horizon relationships;
- colour-space correctness;
- tone-mapping stability;
- day/night differences;
- real Sun and Moon directions;
- clear-weather visual quality.

True moving sea-fog banks and detailed low-level fog volumes are deferred.
General maritime haze and rain-driven visibility are in scope.

PRECIPITATION

Support:

- no precipitation;
- drizzle;
- rain;
- heavy rain.

Snow, sleet and hail are not required.

Use at least two perceptual scales:

1. NEAR PRECIPITATION
   - world-space or convincingly world-anchored drops/streaks;
   - readable speed and wind slant;
   - density appropriate to intensity;
   - correct depth and occlusion behaviour where practical;
   - no obvious camera-fixed pattern;
   - no giant drops;
   - no slow snow-like drift.

2. FAR PRECIPITATION
   - rain curtains, volumetric density or another stable approximation;
   - coherent reduction in visibility;
   - continuity with near rain;
   - no visible particle-radius boundary;
   - no cylindrical wall following the camera.

Rain must:

- follow the current wind field or a documented height-adjusted derivative;
- remain spatially correlated with rain-capable cloud or a documented
  precipitation mask;
- fall beneath the storm or overcast structure rather than uniformly from
  unrelated clear-blue gaps;
- remain stable across camera modes;
- work while looking horizontally, upward and downward;
- look plausible against bright sky, dark cloud and dark night;
- avoid white opaque streaks against the storm;
- respond to Sun, Moon, ambient light, lamp and lightning without glowing
  uniformly;
- scale down appropriately on mobile;
- avoid large per-frame allocations.

Do not use persistent camera-lens droplets or a generic wet-lens overlay in this
round.

RAIN–OCEAN INTERACTION

Rain must visibly affect the ocean.

Provide a scalable combination of:

- local impact dimples or small splash crowns;
- expanding ring ripples;
- rain-driven fine normal roughness;
- far-field statistical rain texture;
- local lighting response.

Do not CPU-simulate every raindrop impact over the entire ocean.

Do not add rain ripples to:

- `WaveField` large displacement;
- raft buoyancy sampling;
- orbital water velocity;
- established swell.

Rain impacts are a high-frequency visual surface layer.

They must:

- remain registered to the water parameter space or another documented stable
  water-surface coordinate;
- avoid sliding over displaced waves;
- avoid periodic wrap pops;
- avoid obvious square tiles;
- remain visible but restrained in calm water;
- coexist with foam and whitewater in rough water;
- receive day, sunset, Moon and lightning illumination;
- not resemble boiling water.

Heavy rain may reduce small-scale specular clarity and alter apparent
roughness. It must not globally erase the final deep-ocean optical model.

RAIN, RAFT, SAIL, AND CASTAWAY WETNESS

Feed precipitation into the final material/wetness architecture established by
the graphics round.

If that round provides one shared wetness response, extend it rather than
creating unrelated rainy material clones.

Rain wetness may:

- darken exposed wood;
- increase controlled specular response;
- dampen or darken cloth;
- alter rope and character material response;
- accumulate with rain;
- dry gradually after rain stops.

Keep this bounded.

Do not implement:

- deck puddle fluid simulation;
- streaming runoff;
- volumetric water accumulation;
- cloth saturation mechanics;
- hypothermia;
- character discomfort or health;
- inventory consequences.

Wet materials must not become:

- chrome;
- black plastic;
- uniformly glossy;
- instantly dry at the end of a preset transition.

LIGHTNING

Lightning is a required part of the thunderstorm condition.

Implement a deterministic event controller with:

- stable seeded scheduling;
- irregular rather than metronomic intervals;
- bounded multi-pulse structure where appropriate;
- in-cloud flashes;
- occasional visible distant cloud-to-ocean bolts;
- event position and distance;
- manual developer trigger;
- freeze/reset support.

LIGHTNING VISUAL REQUIREMENTS

A lightning event should coherently affect:

- the originating cloud volume or mass;
- nearby cloud layers;
- direct scene illumination;
- ocean reflection and local glint;
- rain streak visibility;
- raft, sail and castaway materials;
- exposure and bloom;
- the visible bolt, when present.

Do not implement lightning as only a full-screen white rectangle.

Visible bolts should:

- originate plausibly inside or beneath storm cloud;
- terminate at distant ocean for this round;
- use restrained branching;
- have a bright narrow core and limited glow;
- persist for a physically brief, readable interval;
- avoid looking like a static white tree, neon rope or HUD element;
- remain spatially fixed during the flash;
- be visible from relevant cameras without always striking beside the raft.

In-cloud flashes should reveal cloud structure rather than merely recolour the
whole sky.

Exposure must recover smoothly without leaving the scene black or pumping for
several seconds.

Avoid rapid repetitive strobing. A storm can be dramatic without producing a
continuous high-frequency flash sequence.

THUNDER

Add thunder coherently with lightning.

Use:

- strike distance;
- approximately plausible speed-of-sound delay;
- suitable variation in crack, rumble and duration;
- distance-aware level and filtering;
- restrained spatial presentation where supported.

Thunder must not play simultaneously with every distant visible bolt unless
the distance justifies it.

Do not add lightning damage, fire, electrocution, raft impact or gameplay.

- wind strength and gust response;
- light rain;
- heavy rain on open water;
- rain on sail, wood or raft at a restrained level;
- distant thunder;
- closer thunder for nearer diagnostic strikes.

Audio must:

- transition smoothly;
- avoid obvious short-loop repetition;
- avoid clipping when wind, waves, rain and thunder overlap;

- remain coherent across cinematic and embodied modes;
- use presentation time;
- dispose cleanly.
