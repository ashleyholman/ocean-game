BACKGROUND

The previous raft–water coupling round has established:

- A shared local-render-coordinate wave parameter space.
- `WaveField.setOrigin(presentationOriginX, presentationOriginZ)`, used only as
  a local presentation-phase offset.
- `WaveField.setTime(t)`.
- `WaveField.sample(x, z, out)`.
- Exact CPU/GPU temporal parity.
- Gerstner inverse horizontal-displacement sampling.
- Gerstner orbital water velocity.
- Distributed 24-station buoyancy.
- Fixed 240 Hz physics substeps.
- Eight deterministic diagnostic wave presets.
- A substantial `?debug=buoyancy` verification harness.
- GPU height readback for CPU/rendered-surface parity.
- Contact sheets, videos, numerical metrics and worst-case capture tools.

The current world model is already planetary and authoritative:

- `PlanetaryWorld` owns WGS84/ECEF position, voyage distance and time.
- The renderer remains permanently raft-centred at local `(0, 0)`.
- The former planar `worldX`/`worldZ` travel accumulator and 800 m recenter
  mechanism no longer exist.
- Production keeps `WaveField`'s presentation origin at zero. The buoyancy lab
  may vary it diagnostically to verify CPU/GPU phase behaviour, but it is not
  geography and must not become a second travel or position authority.

The current production ocean is visually strong but represents essentially one
moderate sea condition. Its whitecaps and foam are comparatively underdeveloped.

The coupling report also records several real water-rendering defects:

1. `residualWaveSlope()` is evaluated at displaced `vLocal` while vertex phase
   is evaluated at undisplaced `p`, misregistering residual ripple.
2. `residualWaveSlope()` lacks the Gerstner normalisation needed near compressed
   crests.
3. Detail-origin wrapping is inconsistent with q-space periodicity and causes
   normal-detail popping.
4. The foam noise is not periodic and can jump when the diagnostic presentation
   origin crosses its wrap boundary.
5. True-course changes already update the planetary/local transported frame and
   raft presentation, but the current wave headings are baked at construction;
   the new sea-state controls need an explicit, coherent direction contract.

These are in scope for this round.

CENTRAL EXPERIENCE

The ocean should support recognisably different, physically intelligible
conditions rather than merely scaling one fixed wave pattern up and down.

The player should be able to experience:

- Almost dead-flat water.
- A glassy ocean with long, smooth swell.
- Long remote swell under little local wind.
- Fine wind chop with little underlying swell.
- A moderate mixed open sea.
- Two intersecting swell systems.
- A mature wind sea.
- Post-storm conditions with large organised swell but weakening local wind.
- Rough Southern Ocean-like conditions.
- Debug-only extreme conditions near the limits of the wave representation.

Each condition must differ in:

- Wave scale.
- Dominant period.
- Direction.
- Directional spread.
- Crest shape.
- Group structure.
- Fine roughness.
- Whitecap coverage.
- Foam persistence.
- Spray and spindrift.
- Raft motion.

Do not make every condition look like the same ocean with a different amplitude.

   - local wind sea;
   - primary swell;
   - optional secondary swell;
   - high-frequency surface roughness;

2. Wind speed and direction visibly affect:
   - short-wave energy;
   - wave directionality;
   - fine surface roughness;

   - height;

   - persistent foam;

7. Existing buoyancy remains correct for all raft-survivable sea states.

STRICT ARCHITECTURAL CONSTRAINTS

Preserve the established contract:

- `WaveField` remains the canonical large- and medium-scale displacement field.
- CPU and GPU evaluate the same wave components.
- The ocean and raft use the same simulation time.
- CPU sampling remains in local render coordinates.
- Canonical geographic travel must never be injected into local wave phase.
- Diagnostic changes to the presentation-phase origin, including equivalent
  wrapped offsets, must not create wave, detail-normal or foam discontinuities.
- Inverse horizontal displacement remains correct.
- `WaveField.sample()` continues to return at least:
  - surface height or position;
  - normal;
  - local orbital velocity;
  - whatever additional derivatives are required.
- The raft buoyancy implementation remains distributed and force-based.
- Preserve `PlanetaryWorld`, `RaftBuoyancy`, `OvertopSpray`, `SimHandle`, the
  permanently raft-centred renderer, and the existing `?debug=buoyancy` lab.
- Do not introduce a second position, travel-distance or recentering integrator.
- Do not reintroduce target-following springs or arbitrary delays.
- Do not introduce per-preset raft physics constants.
- Do not tune water rendering to conceal flotation errors.
- Do not replace the proven wave architecture merely because a more fashionable
  technique exists.

The main agent is the sole code integrator.

PHASE 2 — DEFINE THE CANONICAL SEA-STATE MODEL

Use a canonical environment state with the following conceptual layers.

A. LOCAL WIND

Parameters:

- wind speed in metres per second;
- wind direction as a true bearing mapped through the existing transported
  local tangent frame, or an equivalently documented local presentation
  direction;
- gustiness;
- optional fetch or wind-sea maturity;
- optional duration/development factor.

Wind speed must not directly set every wave height.

It should influence:

- local short-wave energy;
- dominant local wind-sea period;
- directional alignment;
- directional spread;
- fine roughness;
- whitecap probability;
- spray and spindrift;
- foam advection.

A newly raised wind should be capable of producing roughened small-scale water
before a fully mature large wind sea develops.

B. PRIMARY SWELL

Parameters:

- significant wave height or a clearly documented proxy;
- peak period;
- true/local-tangent direction using the same documented heading convention as
  the wind sea;
- directional spread;
- crest sharpness or steepness;
- groupiness;
- optional decay or age.

This represents remote organised swell and must not automatically rotate when
local wind direction changes.

Direction controls describe physical headings in the current local tangent
presentation. They must use the existing `PlanetaryWorld`/transported-frame
mapping where true bearings are required and must not create another world
position, voyage-distance or recentering model. Primary and secondary swell
remain independent of the local wind after their headings are established.

C. SECONDARY SWELL

Same conceptual parameters as primary swell, independently enabled.

This supports:

- crossing seas;
- confused seas;
- post-storm multi-system conditions;
- one dominant and one weaker swell.

D. CAPILLARY AND HIGH-FREQUENCY ROUGHNESS

This layer affects reflection and fine appearance but should not materially move
the raft.

It should respond strongly to:

- wind speed;
- viewing angle;
- sun angle;
- gustiness.

It should diminish towards a genuinely glassy state under little wind.

E. WHITEWATER STATE

Maintain persistent whitewater state independently from instantaneous wave
height where practical.

Parameters or derived quantities may include:

- generation rate;
- foam age;
- advection velocity;
- decay rate;
- breakup scale;
- wind streaking;
- spray threshold.

The precise internal representation may differ, but it must not be a static
white mask glued to every crest.

PHASE 3 — WAVE REPRESENTATION

Prefer an expanded deterministic Gerstner-spectrum or hybrid solution unless
the audit proves it inadequate.

A suitable approach may:

- keep a fixed maximum uniform capacity;
- organise components into wind-sea, primary-swell and secondary-swell groups;
- derive component wavelengths and amplitudes around peak periods;
- use deterministic seeded phase and directional distributions;
- use deep-water dispersion;
- use directional spreading around each system’s heading;
- support smooth parameter interpolation;
- expose analytic derivatives and orbital velocity;
- preserve inverse horizontal-displacement sampling.

Do not simply create:

- one large sine wave;
- one small sine wave;
- a global amplitude multiplier.

Each system should contain enough components to avoid visibly repeating regular
patterns.

Use deterministic generation so the same seed and parameters reproduce the
same sea exactly.

If the current fixed eight slots are insufficient:

- increase capacity carefully;
- measure the performance cost;
- avoid shader recompilation when switching ordinary presets;
- retain a practical mobile fallback;
- document why the selected count is sufficient.

If using multiple bands or grouped sums, ensure the CPU and GPU use identical
parameters and equations.

PHASE 4 — CORRECT EXISTING WATER-RENDERING DEFECTS

Fix the known defects from the coupling report.

A. RESIDUAL-WAVE COORDINATES

Ensure residual slope/detail evaluation is spatially registered to the same
undisplaced or correctly inverted surface coordinate used by the main wave
evaluation.

Do not evaluate one layer in displaced coordinates and another in undisplaced
coordinates without a deliberate and documented mapping.

B. GERSTNER NORMAL CORRECTION

Correct residual slope composition around compressed Gerstner crests.

Use the actual surface Jacobian or an equivalent physically coherent derivative
composition.

Do not merely add unnormalised height-field slopes where horizontal displacement
has materially changed surface spacing.

C. PRESENTATION-ORIGIN AND WRAP CONTINUITY

Fix detail and foam coordinate wrapping so:

- detail normals are periodic in the space in which they are evaluated;
- foam does not jump at a 256 m boundary;
- deliberately changing the diagnostic presentation-phase origin produces no
  visible discontinuity at equivalent wrapped coordinates;
- large positive and negative diagnostic origins remain stable;
- wind-streak orientation remains continuous.

Test values immediately below and above every relevant wrap boundary and
equivalent phase origins separated by whole wrap periods. Confirm separately
that canonical geographic travel never drives this presentation origin.

D. WIND-HEADING CONSISTENCY

Changing wind direction in the ocean lab must update:

- local wind-sea directions;
- surface roughness orientation;
- foam advection;
- spindrift direction;
- wind-driven raft drift and yaw where currently applicable.

It must not rotate independently established remote swell unless explicitly
requested by the control.

PHASE 5 — GROUPINESS AND NATURAL VARIATION

Large swell should arrive in perceptible wave groups rather than as perfectly
uniform repetition.

Implement controlled amplitude modulation or spectral grouping that:

- produces sets of somewhat larger and smaller waves;
- remains deterministic;
- avoids obvious periodic pulsing;
- does not break CPU/GPU parity;
- does not cause sudden parameter discontinuities;
- does not resemble a single artificial envelope sine wave.

Allow independent groupiness control for swell systems.

Do not overdo the effect. Ordinary moderate sea should remain natural rather
than theatrical.

PHASE 6 — WHITEWATER AND FOAM

Treat whitewater as a major visual system.

Separate at least:

1. Ocean crest whitecaps.
2. Persistent surface foam.
3. Wind-blown spindrift and spray.
4. Raft overtopping and raft-contact effects.

Do not merge all four into one particle effect.

A. WHITEcap GENERATION

Generate whitecaps from physically meaningful indicators, potentially including:

- local surface steepness;
- crest curvature;
- horizontal compression;
- Jacobian determinant;
- upward velocity;
- crest speed;
- wind speed;
- wind direction relative to wave travel;
- sea maturity.

Do not use a fixed world-height threshold.

Do not paint every crest white.

Calm swell under little wind should generally have little or no whitecap foam,
even when the swell is visually large.

Young steep wind sea should create substantially more whitewater.

B. PERSISTENCE

Foam should survive after initial crest generation.

It should:

- advect with an appropriate approximation of surface water and wind;
- spread and fragment;
- decay over time;
- form irregular patches and streaks;
- vary in age and opacity;
- avoid remaining rigidly fixed to a wave crest;
- remain temporally stable without frame-to-frame flicker.

Possible implementations include:

- low-resolution persistent render targets;
- ping-pong advection textures;
- clipmap-aligned foam fields;
- deterministic procedural history approximations.

Choose a solution compatible with the raft-centred presentation frame,
diagnostic phase-origin changes, wrap-boundary continuity and mobile
performance.

C. FOAM APPEARANCE

Daylight foam should appear:

- bright but not uniformly pure white;
- rough and highly diffuse;
- slightly translucent at thin edges;
- thicker and more opaque in fresh breaking areas;
- structured at multiple scales;
- shadowed and textured enough to avoid looking like flat paint.

Sunlight should make whitewater particularly beautiful.

At sunset and night, foam should remain physically plausible:

- warm highlights at low sun;
- cooler moonlit response;
- no self-illumination unless a later bioluminescence feature is added.

D. SPINDRIFT AND SPRAY

Under strong wind and sufficiently steep crests:

- emit sparse fine spray from crest tops;
- move it downwind;
- vary emission with gusts and whitecap activity;
- keep particles small and short-lived;
- prevent them resembling snow, fog blobs or smoke;
- reduce or disable them in calm conditions.

Do not add heavy volumetric spray in ordinary moderate seas.

E. RAFT EFFECT SEPARATION

Preserve `OvertopSpray.ts` as a raft-specific physical cue.

Do not replace it with general ocean foam.

Future hooks may be added for:

- bow disturbance;
- wake;
- edge splash;
- water-on-deck.

Do not implement a full water-on-deck simulation in this round unless required
to preserve existing overtopping behaviour.

PHASE 8 — SURVIVABILITY CLASSIFICATION

A. COMFORTABLE

B. WET BUT VIABLE

C. MARGINAL

D. UNSURVIVABLE OR DEBUG-ONLY

PHASE 9 — TRANSITIONS

Sea-state parameters must interpolate smoothly.

Support controlled transitions over adjustable simulated durations.

During transitions:

- no phase reset;
- no wave popping;
- no sudden direction snap;
- no abrupt whitecap disappearance;
- persistent foam should decay naturally;
- orbital velocity must remain coherent;
- buoyancy must remain stable;
- the canonical raft-centred presentation must remain stable;
- no diagnostic presentation-origin change may reset phase or foam history.

Changing swell period or component distribution is difficult because replacing
the entire spectrum can pop.

Use a robust strategy such as:

- stable component slots whose parameters morph gradually;
- dual-spectrum crossfading with phase continuity;
- energy-preserving component activation and decay;
- another documented deterministic method.

Do not regenerate all random phases instantaneously.

NON-GOALS AND SCOPE GUARD

Do not implement:

- A global weather simulation.
- Real meteorological forecasts.
- Latitude, longitude, sun, moon or stars.
- Shorelines, reefs or bathymetry.
- Breaking surf on beaches.
- Full Navier–Stokes fluid simulation.
- Volumetric water-on-deck.
- Capsize or structural-breakup gameplay.
- Drowning or survival systems.
- Gameplay-facing technical controls; the explicitly requested
  early-development tools launcher is in scope.
- A complete FFT replacement unless the audit conclusively demonstrates that
  the current architecture cannot meet the acceptance criteria.
- Signed-distance-field ray-marched replacement for the entire ocean.

effect if they clearly outperform conventional geometry or particles and do not
endanger performance or integration.
