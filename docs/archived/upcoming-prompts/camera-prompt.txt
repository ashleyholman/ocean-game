PRIMARY GOAL

Implement two complementary camera modes:

1. A genuinely multi-scale cinematic camera that can move from a relatively close external view to a high, expansive aerial view showing a vastly larger area of ocean.

2. An embodied on-raft camera located approximately at the castaway’s eye position, with unrestricted looking around the raft, up to the zenith, down into the water, and towards the horizon.

Provide a quick, polished manual transition between these modes.

This round is about:

- composition;
- scale;
- embodiment;
- camera interaction;
- maintaining convincing ocean rendering across a much larger viewing range.

It is not a gameplay, swimming, navigation, world-simulation, or character-locomotion round.

CORE EXPERIENCE

The cinematic view should communicate:

> One tiny person and one tiny raft inside an immense ocean.

The embodied view should communicate:

> I am the person on the raft.

The external camera should be capable of producing the kind of vast-ocean impression seen from a low-flying aircraft: the camera gains real altitude, the visible ocean footprint expands substantially, individual waves become visually smaller, and the raft becomes a minute mark in the scene.

Do not constrain the far camera merely because it may eventually reveal information unavailable to the castaway. There are currently no actionable distant objects. Do not introduce artificial fog, event culling, or character-knowledge rules solely to restrict the camera.

Atmospheric haze may still be used for visual realism.

MODE 1 — MULTI-SCALE CINEMATIC CAMERA

The cinematic camera must support a much larger range than the existing approximately 22–60 metres.

The implementation should have a normalised cinematic scale parameter, conceptually:

```ts
cinematicScale01: number; // 0 = close external, 1 = far aerial
```

Use logarithmic or otherwise perceptually well-behaved interpolation.

Do not use a simple linear distance slider if that makes most of the control range visually ineffective.

ZOOM MUST CHANGE BOTH DISTANCE AND ALTITUDE

The current problem is not merely that the raft remains too large.

At far zoom, the player must see a materially larger area of ocean.

As cinematic scale increases:

- horizontal distance must increase;
- altitude must increase substantially;
- camera position elevation may increase;
- optical pitch and framing target may change;
- distant wave detail should attenuate appropriately;
- the raft should occupy dramatically less screen space.

Do not implement zoom-out as predominantly horizontal movement.

TARGET RANGE

Treat these as initial tuning targets, not arbitrary constants:

Close external view:

- camera-to-raft distance approximately 6–12 metres;
- altitude approximately 2.5–6 metres;
- raft large enough for detailed external inspection;
- no camera collision with mast, sail, or character.

Default cinematic view:

- approximately similar to the strongest integrated pre-camera composition;
- roughly 25–45 metres away;
- roughly 8–18 metres altitude;
- clear horizon;
- raft readable but subordinate to ocean and sky.

Medium vista:

- approximately 100–300 metres away;
- approximately 40–120 metres altitude;
- individual waves visibly smaller;
- substantially more ocean surface visible.

Far aerial view:

- target approximately 600–1,200 metres camera distance;
- target approximately 200–450 metres altitude;
- raft becomes a tiny but still discernible mark;
- ocean dominates;
- visible water footprint is vastly greater than at the old 60-metre limit.

The final maximum should be determined through actual browser screenshots and ocean-rendering limits.

Minimum acceptance:

- maximum camera distance must be at least 600 metres;
- maximum altitude must be at least 180 metres;
- the far view must visibly reveal much more ocean surface, not merely a smaller raft.

Prefer reaching approximately 800–1,000 metres distance and 250–350 metres altitude if the existing renderer can be made stable at that range.

RAFT SCALE

Do not artificially enlarge the raft at far distance.

At the farthest useful 4K composition, the raft may be only several pixels across.

Tune the maximum range so the raft is:

- extremely small;
- still locatable;
- not replaced with an oversized icon;
- not billboard-scaled;
- not surrounded by a UI marker in normal mode.

CINEMATIC COMPOSITION

The camera should orbit around the raft but should not be rigidly attached to its pitch and roll.

Use smooth damping.

The camera should follow the raft’s horizontal/local anchor while remaining visually stabilised against rapid wave motion.

Do not rely solely on:

```ts
camera.lookAt(raftPosition);
```

Camera position and optical aim should be decoupled where necessary.

At close and default scales:

- preserve a strong horizon;
- keep the raft in the lower-middle portion of the frame;
- allow substantial sky;
- avoid a sailing-simulator chase-camera composition.

At far aerial scales:

- allow the horizon to move nearer the top of the frame;
- allow ocean to occupy most of the image;
- preserve at least a thin band of sky in the default far composition;
- allow the user to orbit toward a more top-down view if desired.

The default far composition should remain cinematic rather than becoming a flat orthographic map.

CINEMATIC ORBIT

Required controls:

Desktop:

- pointer drag: orbit around raft;
- wheel: cinematic scale/zoom;
- no unrestricted free panning away from the raft.

Touch:

- one-finger drag: orbit;
- pinch: cinematic scale/zoom.

Suggested elevation freedom:

- allow a much broader elevation range than the current system;
- permit low horizon-level views;
- permit high bird’s-eye views;
- do not allow the camera below the water;
- it is acceptable for a deliberately steep user-controlled view to lose the horizon;
- the default framing at each scale should remain composed.

Retain the user’s azimuth while zooming.

Zooming should not unexpectedly rotate the scene.

CINEMATIC CAMERA INVARIANTS

- No NaN or infinity at any scale.
- No abrupt jumps in position, altitude, pitch, target, or FOV.
- Zoom curve is monotonic.
- Altitude increases materially with scale.
- Camera does not cross the ocean surface.
- Camera does not clip through the raft at the close limit.
- Raft remains the orbit anchor.
- Camera does not inherit full raft roll or pitch.
- Resize and orientation changes preserve a valid composition.

MODE 2 — EMBODIED ON-RAFT CAMERA

Implement a separate on-raft viewing mode located approximately at the castaway’s eye position.

This is an embodied viewing camera, not yet a complete first-person character controller.

Required abilities:

- look through a full 360° horizontally;
- look straight up to the zenith;
- look down at the raft and water;
- look towards the horizon;
- inspect mast, sail, rope, logs, and ocean;
- see the real astronomical star field overhead;
- observe the Sun and Moon from raft level.

Do not implement:

- walking;
- crawling;
- shuffling around the raft;
- visible first-person hands;
- swimming;
- jumping;
- body interaction;
- inventory use;
- camera-controlled bird movement;
- first-person gameplay mechanics.

EMBODIED CAMERA ANCHOR

Inspect the current castaway pose.

Place the camera at a plausible eye position for that pose.

If the character is seated or reclining, do not arbitrarily place the camera at standing eye height.

A likely starting point is approximately:

- 1.0–1.4 metres above the raft deck;
- slightly aft of the mast;
- slightly to one side of the mast or sail;
- positioned so the initial view opens onto ocean rather than directly into cloth.

Create an authored local eye anchor rather than inferring the position from an unstable animated vertex.

The embodied camera should move with the raft’s presentation pose.

SAIL OCCLUSION

The sail is real physical geometry and must block the view when the player looks through it.

Do not:

- fade the sail;
- make it transparent;
- hide it solely because it obstructs the camera;
- render it only from one side;
- magically move it away from the player.

The default embodied look direction should avoid placing the entire sail in the centre of the screen.

When the sail is raised:

- the sail may obscure part of the sky and horizon;
- this is expected.

When the sail is lowered:

- the corresponding view should naturally open.

The existing sail-up/sail-down action must continue to function in both camera modes.

CHARACTER-MESH CLIPPING

The embodied camera must not render from inside the castaway’s head.

Use an appropriate technique such as:

- camera visibility layers;
- hiding the head or entire character only in embodied mode;
- a dedicated first-person visibility mask.

Do not build new first-person body geometry in this round.

Document the chosen compromise.

HEAD STABILISATION

A rigid camera inheriting every degree of raft roll and pitch is likely to be unpleasant.

Implement a configurable head-stabilisation model.

The embodied camera should:

- inherit the raft’s translational heave sufficiently to feel physically present;
- inherit some low-frequency pitch and roll;
- attenuate high-frequency or excessive angular motion;
- remain visibly connected to the raft;
- avoid looking like a perfectly horizon-locked floating drone;
- avoid severe motion sickness.

The player’s independent look yaw and pitch should remain stable while the raft moves.

Suggested debug controls:

- roll-follow fraction;
- pitch-follow fraction;
- heave-follow fraction;
- stabilisation smoothing.

Do not expose these controls in normal mode.

EMBODIED LOOK INPUT

Desktop:

- pointer drag to look;
- optionally support pointer lock only if it clearly improves the experience;
- do not require pointer lock for basic use.

Touch:

- one-finger drag to look.

Pitch range:

- approximately −85° downward to +89° upward;
- allow direct zenith viewing;
- avoid mathematical singularities or yaw inversion near the poles of the view sphere.

Embodied mode should use a stable FOV appropriate for human-scale observation, likely around 55–70° vertical depending on the existing renderer.

Do not use wheel zoom in embodied mode unless there is a clearly justified, subtle FOV adjustment. A fixed embodied FOV is acceptable and preferable to adding unnecessary controls.

MODE SWITCHING

Provide an explicit manual mode toggle.

Use:

- `V` by default, unless it conflicts with an existing control;
- a developer-panel mode selector;
- an optional unobtrusive temporary control hint.

The mode switch must preserve:

- the last cinematic azimuth;
- the last cinematic elevation;
- the last cinematic scale;
- embodied look yaw and pitch where sensible.

Returning to cinematic mode should restore the prior cinematic composition rather than resetting arbitrarily.

CAMERA TRANSITION

Implement a quick cinematic transition between the modes.

Target duration:

- approximately 0.7–1.4 seconds;
- configurable in debug mode.

The transition should:

- use smooth ease-in/ease-out;
- avoid instantaneous teleportation;
- avoid moving through mast, sail, character, or raft;
- work with sail raised and lowered;
- work from close, default, and far cinematic scales;
- end precisely at the destination camera state;
- suppress conflicting camera input while blending;
- not mutate canonical world state.

A safe spline or staged path may be used:

- cinematic position;
- safe point above/aft of raft;
- embodied eye anchor.

When transitioning out from a very distant aerial view, the camera may move rapidly, but the movement must remain visually comprehensible and not produce a severe disorienting spin.

If a direct transition from the maximum aerial distance is visually poor, use a deliberately authored multi-stage dolly rather than silently reducing the supported range.

Do not add an automatic idle-to-cinematic transition in this round.

Leave an explicit hook for a future watch/idle mode, but manual switching is the only required behaviour now.

At far aerial range, ensure:

- no visible edge to the ocean;
- no obvious square or circular mesh boundary;

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

The round is complete only when all of the following are true:

1. Two explicit camera modes exist:
   - cinematic;
   - embodied on-raft.

2. Cinematic zoom extends substantially beyond the old 60-metre limit.

3. Maximum cinematic distance is at least 600 metres.

4. Maximum cinematic altitude is at least 180 metres.

5. Zoom-out materially increases visible ocean area.

6. The far view makes the raft extremely small and communicates oceanic vastness.

7. The raft is not artificially enlarged or replaced with an icon.

8. The cinematic camera supports close external inspection substantially nearer than the old 22-metre limit.

9. Cinematic orbit supports low horizon-level and high bird’s-eye compositions.

10. The default far view remains visually composed and does not accidentally become a broken top-down map.

11. The embodied camera is placed at a plausible castaway eye position.

12. The embodied camera supports full horizontal look.

13. The embodied camera can look directly to the zenith.

14. The embodied camera can look down at the raft and surrounding water.

15. The sail physically occludes the embodied view.

16. Lowering the sail naturally clears that obstruction.

17. No camera renders from inside the castaway’s head.

18. Head stabilisation feels connected to the raft without producing severe motion sickness.

19. Manual mode switching works with a quick cinematic transition.

20. Transition works from near, default, and far cinematic scales.

21. Transition does not visibly pass through the sail, mast, raft, or character.

22. Last cinematic composition is restored after leaving embodied mode.

23. Existing sail controls function in both camera modes.

24. Existing Sun, Moon direction, stars, and astronomy remain correct.

25. The ocean has no visible edge at the supported far range.

26. Distant water does not exhibit severe repetition, shimmer, moiré, or implausibly large detail.

27. Camera actions do not mutate canonical planetary state.

28. Camera motion uses presentation time rather than accelerated world time.

29. Desktop and touch controls work.

30. 4K, standard desktop, mobile portrait, and mobile landscape are usable.

31. All existing tracked planetary, raft-water, sea-state, CPU/GPU parity and
    diagnostic-lab tests continue to pass.

32. New camera tests pass.

33. Type checking passes.

34. Production build passes.

35. Fresh loads and every tools-panel/deep-link path produce no material console
    errors.

36. The visible developer-tools launcher exposes World/General, Buoyancy, Ocean
    and Camera without requiring query parameters; `Hide all` produces a clean
    view with no permanent camera HUD or open debug panel.

37. No major out-of-scope gameplay system was added.

38. The final visual review has no unresolved severity-one camera or ocean-scale defect.

39. Existing debug deep links remain operable and open the same shared panels as
    the visible launcher.

40. Representative calm, moderate and rough sea states remain physically and
    visually coherent in close, default, far and embodied views.

41. No final-round lighting, atmosphere, colour-grading, water-material or
    general graphics redesign has been smuggled into this camera round.
