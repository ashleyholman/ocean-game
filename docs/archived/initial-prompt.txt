You are the lead real-time graphics engineer, interaction designer and visual director for a small experimental web game.

Build the working application. Do not merely provide a design document or code excerpts.

PROJECT NAME
Drift — Atmospheric Ocean Prototype 0.1

CORE EXPERIENCE

The page should create an immediate feeling of romanticised solitude: one tiny human on a crude wooden raft, surrounded by an apparently limitless ocean at sunset.

This prototype is not a sailing simulator, survival game, crafting game or management game. It should not contain technical nautical controls, resources, meters, objectives, quests, inventory, health, hunger, maps or navigation systems.

The visual hierarchy must be:

1. Vast ocean and sky.
2. Changing light, waves and atmosphere.
3. A very small raft and human figure.
4. Almost no interface.

The player should feel that the world is immense and autonomous, and that the raft is only a minute presence within it.

SCOPE

This pass is only an atmospheric visual and interaction prototype.

Implement:

- A beautiful animated ocean.
- A coherent sunset-to-night sky.
- A small wooden raft moving convincingly with the waves.
- A tiny human figure for scale and emotional presence.
- A distant oblique camera that preserves the horizon.
- User-controlled camera orbit and zoom.
- One interaction: raise or lower a crude rag sail.
- Simple drift caused by wind when the sail is raised.
- Responsive desktop and mobile presentation.

Do not implement:

- A radio.
- Islands or land.
- Ships, whales, birds or encounters.
- Storms or multiple weather states.
- Fishing.
- Paddling or steering.
- Survival mechanics.
- Inventory or collectible objects.
- A map or compass.
- Saving or offline progression.
- Multiplayer.
- Narrative text.
- A title menu.
- Any permanent HUD.
- Additional gameplay systems simply because they seem conventional.

The purpose is to discover whether merely inhabiting this scene is compelling.

TECHNOLOGY

If the repository is empty, initialise a minimal Vite + TypeScript application using Three.js.

Prefer:

- Plain TypeScript rather than React unless the existing repository already requires React.
- Three.js with WebGL rendering.
- Modular, readable source files.
- Strict TypeScript.
- No heavyweight physics engine.
- Procedural geometry and shaders where practical.
- Permissively licensed or CC0 assets only.
- An ASSET_CREDITS.md file for every external texture, model, sound or environment asset.

Suggested source structure:

Do not create a large hierarchy of agents. Do not allow agents to add features

CAMERA AND COMPOSITION

Use a wide, elevated, three-quarter observer camera.

The default composition should approximately use:

- Camera distance from raft: 30–45 metres.
- Camera height above water: 10–16 metres.
- Vertical field of view: approximately 45–52 degrees.
- Downward viewing angle: approximately 15–25 degrees.
- Horizon positioned near the upper third of the frame.
- Raft occupying approximately 8–12% of viewport width.

These are starting values. Adjust them based on actual screenshots.

The camera must not be:

- Top-down.
- First-person.
- Closely attached behind the raft.
- Rigidly parented to the raft.
- Constantly rotating in a distracting way.

The camera should follow the raft’s horizontal world position using smooth
spring damping. It should remain substantially stabilised against the raft’s
pitch and roll, with only extremely subtle vertical movement.

Controls:

- Pointer drag or one-finger drag: orbit around the raft.
- Mouse wheel or pinch: zoom.
- Disable free panning away from the raft.
- Clamp camera elevation so the horizon is always visible.
- Prevent the camera going below the water.
- Suggested orbit elevation range: 7–30 degrees above the horizontal.
- Suggested distance range: 20–65 metres.

After approximately 20 seconds without camera input, allow a very gentle
cinematic drift of no more than about 10–15 degrees over 30 seconds. Stop this
immediately when the player interacts. Do not perform a perpetual full orbit.

Frame the raft in the lower-middle area rather than filling the centre of the
screen. Ocean and sky must dominate every composition.

OCEAN

Create an ocean that looks natural and materially convincing rather than like
a flat blue plane or swimming pool.

Use a custom animated water surface or an equivalent high-quality approach
with:

- Several broad Gerstner-style wave components or another coherent wave model.
- Moderate swell suitable for a small raft.
- Approximate broad wave amplitude of 0.3–0.7 metres in the default scene.
- Smaller high-frequency normal detail.
- Fresnel reflection.
- View-dependent specular highlights.
- A visible trail of reflected sunlight across the water.
- Subtle moonlight reflection after sunset.
- Atmospheric fading and haze towards the horizon.
- Sparse, restrained foam or whitecaps where appropriate.
- Natural deep-blue and slate-blue water rather than tropical turquoise.

Avoid exaggerated storm waves in this version.

The ocean should appear effectively infinite. Re-centre or tile the surface as
needed so the player cannot see an edge. Avoid precision problems during
drift.

The wave system must expose a CPU-side sampling function, or an equivalent
method, so the raft can use the same wave definition as the rendered water.

RAFT MOVEMENT

The raft must visibly belong to the water rather than floating through an
unrelated animation.

Sample wave height and surface orientation at multiple positions beneath the
raft. Use those samples to calculate:

- Vertical position.
- Pitch.
- Roll.

Apply damping so the response has mass and does not jitter.

Do not rigidly copy a single wave normal. Use at least three sample points or
another robust method that produces convincing orientation.

The raft should slowly drift across the hidden world. It may remain near the
render origin while world offsets are accumulated internally.

RAFT DESIGN

Build a small, visually credible improvised raft, approximately 3.2 by
2.2 metres:

- Uneven weathered logs or heavy planks.
- Visible rope lashings.
- Imperfect construction.
- One crude mast.
- One faded, patched, off-white rag sail.
- No polished yacht components.
- No elaborate equipment.
- No visible engine.
- No technical instrument panel.

Include one small seated or reclining human figure, approximately 1.75 metres
tall, primarily as a silhouette and scale reference.

Do not spend excessive time producing a detailed face or character model. A
tasteful, low-detail figure is preferable to an uncanny high-detail one.

SAIL INTERACTION

Spacebar, or tapping/clicking the sail or mast, toggles the sail between raised
and lowered.

Animate the transition over roughly 1–1.5 seconds.

When raised:

- The sail should have subtle cloth deformation and flutter.
- The raft’s drift should gradually accelerate in the fixed wind direction.
- Suggested target drift speed: approximately 0.4–0.7 metres per second.

When lowered:

- Show the cloth gathered or rolled near the mast.
- The raft should retain a much slower background drift.
- Suggested drift speed: approximately 0.05–0.15 metres per second.

There is no steering, trimming, tacking, port/starboard control or optimisation.
The sail is deliberately binary: up or down.

The wind determines the direction. The player does not.

SKY, SUNSET AND NIGHT

Construct a coherent world-space sky rather than applying a simple
screen-space gradient.

The western direction should contain:

- A low sun close to the horizon.
- Warm, natural gold and subdued orange light.
- Thin illuminated clouds.
- A reflected sun path across the water.

The opposite side of the world should transition into:

- Cooler indigo and deep blue.
- The first visible stars.
- A moon or moon glow once the sun has set sufficiently.
- Noticeably darker water and sky.

Do not force the western sunset and eastern night sky into one narrow camera
view. They exist in different world directions. The player discovers the
contrast by orbiting around the raft.

The default experience should begin shortly before sunset and transition
gradually into early night over approximately 8–12 real minutes.

Implement:

- Continuous sun movement.
- Directional light and colour-temperature change.
- Atmospheric scattering or a convincing approximation.
- Stars fading in based on solar elevation.
- Moonlight fading in gradually.
- Exposure adjustments that avoid abrupt changes.
- Clouds that remain subtle and do not cover the entire sky.

The effect should be cinematic but physically plausible enough to avoid
looking like a generic oversaturated fantasy wallpaper.

Avoid:

- Excessive bloom.
- Neon purple shadows.
- Extreme orange-and-teal grading.
- Heavy lens flares.
- Chromatic aberration.
- Artificially dense stars while the sun is still bright.

Provide a developer-only tuning panel enabled by `?debug=1`. It may expose:

- Time of day.
- Time progression speed.
- Sun direction.
- Wave amplitude.
- Wind direction and strength.
- Camera distance.
- Exposure.

The debug interface must never appear during normal use.

LIGHTING AND RENDERING

Use appropriate colour management and cinematic tone mapping.

Recommended qualities:

- Physically coherent sun direction.
- Soft ambient sky illumination.
- ACES-style filmic tone mapping or an equivalent restrained method.
- Subtle bloom only around genuinely bright light sources.
- High-quality anti-aliasing where practical.
- Soft atmospheric haze at the horizon.
- Correct sRGB output.
- Dynamic pixel ratio capped to protect performance.

The raft should receive warm sunset light on one side and cool ambient light
on the other. It must not appear pasted onto the scene.

AUDIO

Visual quality takes priority.

After the visual scene is stable, add minimal ocean and wind ambience only if
it can be done cleanly with a permissively licensed asset or a convincing
procedural implementation.

Because browsers may block autoplay, begin audio after the first user
interaction while allowing the visual scene to animate immediately.

Provide `M` as a mute toggle.

Do not delay completion of the prototype for audio.

INTERFACE

The scene should render immediately without a title screen.

A small translucent hint may appear temporarily:

“Drag to look · Pinch or scroll to zoom · Space to raise sail”

Fade it out completely after several seconds.

Do not add:

- Buttons around the edge.
- Numerical readouts.
- Coordinates.
- Icons.
- A minimap.
- A clock.
- A settings panel in normal mode.
- A tutorial window.
- A logbook.
- Decorative UI frames.

PERFORMANCE

Target:

- Smooth rendering on a modern desktop at 1440p.
- At least a usable 30 frames per second at a mobile-sized viewport.
- Responsive layout in landscape and portrait.
- Touch camera controls.
- No enormous textures or unnecessary geometry.
- No unbounded object allocation inside the animation loop.

Cap device pixel ratio where appropriate and reduce expensive effects
gracefully on smaller devices.

QUALITY REVIEW

After the first implementation:

1. Run the application in a real browser.
2. Capture screenshots at approximately:
   - 1440 × 900 desktop.
   - 390 × 844 mobile portrait.
   - At least two different camera directions.
   - Sunset and early-night states.
3. Give those screenshots to the visual-critic subagent.
4. Fix the critic’s highest-severity findings.
5. Repeat visual review for no more than three focused passes.

Do not allow the critic to request additional gameplay systems.

The critic should specifically reject:

- A raft that is too large.
- A camera that resembles a sailing simulator.
- A flat or obviously tiled ocean.
- A missing or unconvincing horizon.
- Water motion not reflected in raft motion.
- Oversaturated lighting.
- Excessive UI.
- A sky that looks like a static background image.
- Mobile framing that crops out the horizon.
- An uncanny or dominant character model.

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

The prototype is complete only when:

1. Opening the page immediately communicates “one tiny person drifting on a
   raft in an immense ocean.”
2. The horizon is visible in the default view.
3. The raft is small but still readable.
4. The ocean has convincing large-scale motion and fine surface detail.
5. The raft visibly rises, pitches and rolls with the same waves.
6. Camera orbit reveals a spatially coherent sunset side and darker
   star-emerging side of the world.
7. Raising the sail visibly changes the raft and modestly increases drift,
   without introducing sailing controls.
8. After the temporary instruction fades, the normal scene has no visible UI.
9. Desktop and mobile layouts remain usable.
10. The production build succeeds with no material errors.
